Teddy Bear Cut or Scissor Trim? What Pomeranian Owners Ask Groomers For

A count of real owner requests from 2026, the breed standard wording, and a brief you can copy for your next appointment.

The Pomeranian teddy bear cut and the scissor trim sound similar at the salon desk, yet owners describe very different results when they pick up their dog. One name can mean a light tidy to one groomer and a short all-over cut to another. We read seven 2026 owner discussions, counted what people actually ask for, and turned the most repeated advice into a groomer brief you can fill in below.

Quick answer: Of 21 owners and groomers who stated a preference, 9 (43%) asked for a scissor trim or outline trim, 7 (33%) kept the full coat and only tidied the feet, rear and ears, and 5 (24%) chose a teddy bear or "puppy" cut. The two owners who gave a length talked about roughly 1.5–2 inches on the body. The most repeated briefing tips were to bring a photo, say "trim, not shave", and ask the groomer to call before clipping anything short.

What do Pomeranian owners actually ask groomers for?

Most owners in our count asked for a scissor or outline trim, and about a third kept the full coat with only small tidy-ups. A teddy bear or puppy cut was the least common choice, picked by roughly one owner in four.

We read seven r/Pomeranians discussions posted between January and November 2026: threads about grooming routines, cuts that came back too short, whether trimming is a good idea and what to ask a salon. We counted one preference per person and only when the comment said what they ask for or do, not when they only reacted to a photo.

A comment went into the table only if it stated a preference or a routine. People who described two dogs were counted once, by the cut they described in more detail. With 21 people this is a snapshot of what engaged owners say, not a survey of every Pomeranian household, and we could not check the haircuts themselves.

Length: only two owners gave a number

Owners rarely talk in inches. One owner wrote that they would not go "shorter than 2 inches" when trimming for warm weather. Another described a teddy-style cut that was "not really a shave but 1.5–2 inch long". Everyone else used words such as "a little shorter", "not super short" or "keep him long". That vagueness is exactly why the brief builder asks you to pick a length.

What is the difference between a teddy bear cut and a scissor trim?

A scissor trim shapes the outline of the coat and leaves most of its length, while a teddy bear cut shortens the coat all over and rounds the face and feet into a plush, bear-like shape. The names overlap, so describe the length you want instead of relying on the name.

Why the same name gives different results

One owner whose dog came back far shorter than expected was told by another commenter that "it looks like the groomer did a teddy bear", the cut that commenter's own dogs get regularly. A third described a cut like that as about 1.5–2 inches long. To one person a teddy bear is a light rounding; to another it is a short, even all-over cut. Naming the length removes most of that guesswork.

What does the breed standard say about trimming?

The breed standard allows a tidy outline but describes a long, standing-off coat. The AKC Pomeranian standard says "Trimming for neatness and a clean outline is permissible."

The same AKC text describes a double coat: a short, dense undercoat with long, harsh-textured guard hair that forms an abundant outer coat standing off from the body, a ruff around the neck, and shorter, tightly packed coat on the head and legs. The Royal Kennel Club breed standard describes the outer coat as "long, perfectly straight, harsh in texture and covering whole of body", very abundant around the neck and chest.

What that means for a pet trim

Breed standards are written for judging show dogs, not for telling you how your pet must look. They are still useful vocabulary: "a clean outline" is close to what owners mean by a scissor or outline trim. A short teddy bear cut is a pet style that moves away from the coat the standards describe, and that is a choice many owners make knowingly.

Why do Pomeranian cuts come out shorter than owners expected?

In these threads the usual causes were vague instructions, a new groomer, and mats that were dealt with by clipping. At least six owners described a shave or a much shorter cut than they had asked for.

The three patterns owners described

  • A different groomer. Several bad results happened at a new salon or with a groomer who had not seen the dog before. A groomer in one thread advised prebooking with your regular groomer so you are not handed to someone new.
  • A mix-up or a shortcut. One owner said their dog went in for a trim and was mixed up with another Pom booked for a shave. Another said it had happened to them twice despite bringing photos and asking for a scissor cut.
  • Mats. Groomers and owners agreed that when the coat is matted close to the skin, clipping can be the only practical option. One groomer said an experienced groomer will show you the problem areas and have you sign off before shaving.

Mats change the conversation

If your Pom arrives with mats, the groomer's choice is no longer only about style. Owners repeatedly recommended line brushing between visits to keep the cut you want on the table. Our guide to how often owners brush, bathe and book the groomer shows what owners do between appointments, and the harness straps are a common mat spot, as owners noted in our harness training guide.

How to brief your groomer, step by step

These steps combine the tips owners repeated most often. None of them needs special vocabulary.

  1. Bring a photo. Three owners said a picture of the cut they like is the single most useful thing to hand over. Keep it on your phone and show it at every drop-off.
  2. Say the length, not only the name. "Teddy bear, about 2 inches on the body" leaves far less room than "teddy bear".
  3. Use the word "trim". One owner's rule: always say you want a trim, not a shave-down, and ask whether the groomer knows how to trim a Pomeranian.
  4. Ask for a call before anything short. Owners asked groomers to call first if they wanted to clip anything short. One owner said to get it in writing so there is no "he said, she said".
  5. List the small areas. Sanitary area, feet and pads, ear tips, tail. These are the parts owners most often wanted tidied even when the body stays long.
  6. Check at drop-off. Ask the groomer to look over the coat with you for knots behind the ears, armpits and rear, so any change of plan is discussed before you leave.
  7. Stay with one groomer. Owners who found a groomer they trusted kept rebooking the same person.

If your Pom is still a puppy

A first salon visit goes more smoothly when the puppy is already used to being handled and brushed. Our week-by-week puppy brushing routine builds that habit at home before the coat needs much trimming.

Common mistakes when booking a Pom cut

  • Booking by name only. "Teddy bear" and "puppy cut" mean different lengths at different salons.
  • Leaving the length to the groomer. "Groomer's choice" is fine only if you are happy with any outcome.
  • Arriving with mats and asking for full length. The two requests may not be compatible on the day. Brush out knots beforehand or expect the groomer to discuss options.
  • Not saying what to leave alone. If you want the ruff or tail untouched, say so in the brief, next to the list of areas to tidy.
  • Switching salons often. Several unwanted cuts in these threads came from a groomer who did not know the dog.

Frequently asked questions

What is a Pomeranian teddy bear cut?

It is a pet style where the body coat is shortened to an even length and the face and feet are rounded into a plush shape. The length varies by salon, so state it, for example about 2 inches on the body.

Is a scissor trim the same as a teddy bear cut?

Not usually. A scissor or outline trim shapes the edges and keeps most of the length; a teddy bear cut shortens the whole coat. Some groomers do a teddy bear style with scissors, which is why describing the length matters more than the name.

Which cut do most Pomeranian owners choose?

In our count of 21 owners and groomers in 2026 discussions, 43% asked for a scissor or outline trim, 33% kept the full coat with small tidy-ups and 24% chose a teddy bear or puppy cut.

How short is too short for a Pomeranian trim?

Owners rarely gave numbers. The two who did described roughly 1.5–2 inches on the body as their shortest regular trim. Shaving close to the skin was something owners in these threads mostly accepted only when matting left no alternative.

Does the breed standard allow trimming?

Yes, within limits. The AKC Pomeranian standard says trimming for neatness and a clean outline is permissible, while describing a long, abundant outer coat that stands off from the body.

What should I tell the groomer at drop-off?

Show a photo, name the length, list the small areas to tidy, say what to leave alone and ask for a call before anything is clipped short. The brief builder at the top of this page puts all of that into one short note.

The cut you want starts with the words you use

Whether you choose a Pomeranian teddy bear cut or a scissor trim, the result depends less on the name than on how clearly you describe it. Most owners in our count asked for a scissor outline and kept the coat long; a smaller group liked a teddy bear look at around 1.5–2 inches. Bring a photo, give a length, ask for a call before anything short, and keep the coat brushed so that the cut you asked for is still possible on the day.
