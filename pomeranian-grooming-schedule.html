How Often Pomeranian Owners Really Brush, Bathe and Book the Groomer

Owner-reported routines from 2026, side by side with the official breed advice.

A Pomeranian grooming schedule looks simple on a breed page and messy in real homes. The American Kennel Club's advice fits in a single paragraph, yet when owners compare notes, one Pom gets a bath every week while another gets one every three months. We sat down with the actual routines that owners shared publicly in 2026 and laid them next to the breed advice, so you can judge your own schedule against both.

Quick answer: The AKC breed page says brush down to the skin once a week and book a full groom every four to six weeks if you don't do it at home. Owners report doing more brushing and fewer salon visits. In our tally, 62% of dogs were brushed every one to three days, the median bath came about every 4–5 weeks, and owners who used a professional groomer went every 7–8 weeks (median).

Where do these owner numbers come from?

They come from three public r/Pomeranians threads posted between May and August 2026, with nearly 100 comments between them. We counted every reply that gave a concrete frequency, and we counted each dog separately when an owner described different routines for different dogs.

The largest thread, "Please share your Grooming Routine", asked how often Poms are groomed, whether owners do it themselves, and what to ask a salon for. Two later threads asked about bathing frequency and how much brushing a heavily coated Pom really needs. After filtering out photo-only comments and compliments, we ended up with:

  • 29 brushing routines with a stated frequency
  • 28 bathing routines with a stated interval, at home or at a salon
  • 8 professional-groomer routines with a stated interval
  • 8 owners who said they skip the salon and do most or all of the work themselves

Where someone gave a range, such as "every 6–8 weeks", we used the midpoint. "Every 2–3 months" became roughly 10.5 weeks. Three of the replies came from people who said they work as groomers, which matters: two of the shortest intervals in the data (a bath or groom every one to two weeks) belong to groomers' own dogs.

What this tally can't tell you

People who reply to a grooming thread are probably more invested in grooming than average. Nobody verified their claims, and a routine described on a good week may not survive a busy month. Read the percentages as "what dedicated owners report", not as a national average.

How often do owners actually brush a Pomeranian?

Most brush far more often than the once-a-week minimum. In our tally, 10 of 29 dogs (34%) were brushed daily and 8 more (28%) every two to three days, so 62% got a brush-through at least every three days.

The reasons owners gave were remarkably consistent. Mats form in the same places: behind the ears, in the armpits, around the rear and under harness straps. One owner checks behind the ears "almost daily" even though full brushing happens every couple of days. Another brushes the harness area daily because the coat is coarse and "mats quickly under his harness". If your Pom is still getting used to a harness, our gentle harness training plan covers the dressing side; the grooming side is simply checking under those straps after walks.

How long does a session take?

Owners who gave times reported 10–15 minutes, 20–30 minutes per dog, and in one case 30 minutes every evening in front of the TV, a habit started in puppyhood. The once-a-week brushers described the opposite trade-off. One household does "a big ass long brush once a week" and says it works if it is thorough. Another admitted that skipping too long means "everything just gets harder to manage all at once".

Line brushing versus surface brushing

Several owners stressed technique over frequency. A Pom has a double coat: stiff guard hairs over a soft, insulating undercoat, and spitz-type breeds tend to carry a thick undercoat. Brushing only the top layer leaves the undercoat to tangle. That is why the AKC says to brush "down to the skin", and why owners talk about "line brushing from the root" and combing through afterwards. One owner who brushes every few days does a deeper line brush every two to three weeks.

How often do Pomeranian owners bathe their dogs?

The median reported interval was about every 4–5 weeks, but the spread was huge: from weekly to roughly every three months. Bathing is where owner practice varies most, and where no single rule fits everyone.

Why the spread is so wide

Owners who bathe often usually point to the environment: one family in a city adjusts the schedule to weather and dirty streets, and one groomer's dog gets an extra bath after daycare. Owners at the long end, every 2–3 months, tend to brush every day or every other day and spot-clean feet, face and rear in between. One owner moved from a bath every 2 weeks to every 4–5 weeks because they felt the frequent baths were too drying, and keeps a no-rinse shampoo for spot cleaning instead.

Time matters too, and it explains why many people don't bathe more often. Owners described drying a full-coated Pom as "about an hour" for two people, 45 minutes with a hairdryer for an 8 lb dog, and 2–3 hours for the whole bath when the dog is in full coat. Size changes that math. If you are curious how much adult Poms really weigh, see our owner-reported adult weight data.

Spot cleaning fills the gaps

Almost every owner on the long-interval end described a daily or after-walk wipe: face with a warm washcloth, feet with a separate cloth, rear with a damp towel. Our comparison of how long owners walk their Poms shows how much outdoor time varies from home to home.

How often do owners book a professional groomer?

Owners who use a salon reported a median of about every 7–8 weeks, a little longer than the AKC's four-to-six-week suggestion. Many others skip the salon entirely.

The eight salon routines ranged from every 2 weeks to about three times a year. The cluster sat at 6–10 weeks: every 6 weeks, every 6–8 weeks, "every 8 weeks or so", every 8–10 weeks "depending on the time of year", and every 10 weeks with one home bath in between. One owner pays for a groom only about three times a year, mainly to help get the winter coat out.

Why so many owners do it themselves

Eight owners said they handle most or all of the grooming at home. The reason they gave most often was fear of a cut they didn't ask for. Comments such as "terrified of them cutting her too short" and "I've seen too many horror stories of groomers shaving Poms" came up repeatedly. Owners who did use salons offered the counter-advice: be very clear about what you want, bring a photo, and ask the salon to call you before shaving anything.

DIY isn't free of effort. One owner tried YouTube tutorials and a scissor kit, found it "much harder than it looks", and went back to a salon, trying a few before finding one they trust. Another bought shears, combs and a high-velocity dryer and doesn't regret it, because drying with a regular hairdryer had taken about 45 minutes.

Is the official breed advice wrong?

No, it is a minimum written for an average coat, not a full description of how dedicated owners live with the breed. The gap between the advice and the owner reports mostly reflects coat type, lifestyle and how much work each owner wants to hand to a salon.

The Royal Kennel Club's Pomeranian profile lists grooming as "more than once a week" and notes that the breed sheds. That sits closer to what owners actually do than a strict once-weekly reading of the AKC text does. The fair reading of both is that once a week is the floor, and many Poms need more.

Coat type changes everything

The clearest example came from one owner with two dogs on completely different routines. Their boy, with a "verrryy cottony undercoat", gets brushed daily and bathed by a groomer every 2 weeks. Their girl has a silkier coat with little undercoat and gets a home bath every 4–6 weeks and brushing twice a week. Same household, same owner, different coats.

Build your own schedule: a step-by-step checklist

Use this to set a routine you can actually keep, then adjust it after a month based on what you find in the coat.

  1. Start at the floor. Plan at least one full line-brushing session a week, down to the skin, as the AKC describes.
  2. Add hot-spot checks. Every day or two, run a comb behind the ears, through the armpits, around the rear and under harness straps. This takes a few minutes and is where owners say mats start.
  3. Do the comb test. One owner's rule was simple: brush when you can no longer glide your fingers through the coat. If a comb snags in the same spot twice in a week, brush that area more often.
  4. Pick a bath interval from your lifestyle, not a chart. City walking, daycare or mud may push you toward 2–4 weeks; a mostly indoor dog with daily wipes may stretch to 6–8.
  5. Budget drying time. If a full dry takes you an hour, put the bath on a day you have that hour.
  6. Decide what goes to a salon. Some owners pay only for nails, others for a sanitary trim every 6–8 weeks, others for a full groom. Write down exactly what you want before booking.
  7. Plan for shedding seasons. Owners described adjusting groom intervals "depending on the time of year" and booking extra help to remove a winter coat.

Common grooming schedule mistakes owners describe

  • Brushing only the top layer. A fluffy-looking surface can hide tangles near the skin. Line brush and follow with a comb.
  • Saving all brushing for bath day. Owners who skip brushing say everything becomes harder at once.
  • Copying another dog's bath schedule. The two-dog household above shows that coat type can double or halve the right interval.
  • Booking a salon without instructions. The most common salon complaint in the threads was an unexpected short cut. Bring a photo and state "no shaving" if that is what you want.
  • Ignoring the harness line. Several owners found mats exactly where straps rub on walks.
  • Treating a busy-month schedule as failure. The owners with the calmest routines described short, frequent sessions they could keep up, not marathon grooms.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I brush my Pomeranian?

The AKC sets a minimum of one thorough brush down to the skin each week. In our 2026 owner tally, 62% of dogs were brushed every one to three days, and daily checks behind the ears and under harness straps were common.

How often do Pomeranian owners give a bath?

The median in our tally was about every 4–5 weeks. Reported intervals ranged from weekly to roughly every three months, depending on lifestyle, coat type and how much spot cleaning owners do in between.

How often should a Pomeranian go to the groomer?

The AKC suggests a full groom every four to six weeks if you are not doing it at home. Owners in our tally who used a salon went about every 7–8 weeks (median), and eight owners did most of the work themselves.

Can I groom a Pomeranian completely at home?

Many owners do, using pin and slicker brushes, a comb, shears and a strong dryer. Others found trimming harder than expected and returned to a salon. A common middle ground is brushing and bathing at home and paying only for nails or trims.

How long does it take to bathe and dry a Pomeranian?

Owners reported about an hour for drying and brushing out a full-coated Pom, 45 minutes with a regular hairdryer for an 8 lb dog, and up to 2–3 hours for the whole bath when the dog is in full coat.

Where do Pomeranians mat first?

Owners repeatedly named behind the ears, the armpits, the rear and the areas under harness straps. A quick daily comb through those spots prevents most of the work later.

The fair answer: a floor, not a finish line

Your Pomeranian grooming schedule doesn't have to match a breed page or a stranger's routine to be the right one. Use the AKC's weekly brush and 4–6-week groom as the floor, look at what owners with similar coats actually do, and adjust to the mats and dirt you actually find. If the comb glides through and the dog is comfortable, your schedule is working.
