How Much Do Pomeranian Owners Actually Feed? Daily Cups by Dog Weight

Real owner portions from 2026, next to a veterinary calorie chart, in a few simple steps.

Home›Blog›Feeding amount

How much to feed a Pomeranian is one of the most common questions new owners ask, and the answers online can differ several times over. Feeding charts start from the dog's weight; owners on Pomeranian forums start from what keeps their own dog steady. We collected what owners actually put in the bowl, sorted it by dog weight, and set it next to a veterinary calorie chart so you can find a sensible starting point in a few simple steps.

Quick answer: Across 17 owner reports from 2026, the most common daily amount was 1/2 cup of dry food a day, usually split into two 1/4-cup meals, for Poms of about 6–10 lb. The smallest dogs (around 5.5 lb) got about 1/4 cup a day. Your own food's calories per cup decide whether that is right, so check the "Calorie Content" line on the bag before copying anyone's scoop.

How much do Pomeranian owners actually feed per day?

Most owners in our tally fed about 1/2 cup of dry food a day, split into two meals. Eleven of 17 dogs (65%) were within a spoonful of that amount, and the median for the whole group was exactly 1/2 cup.

The numbers come from three r/Pomeranians threads posted in March and August 2026. The biggest, "How much should we be feeding Little Bear?", drew 93 comments. We kept every reply that gave both a dog's weight and a daily amount of dry food. Tablespoons were converted with 16 tablespoons to a US cup, so 2 tablespoons twice a day equals 1/4 cup a day.

What we left out, and why

We removed every report tied to a prescription or veterinary diet, a dog on medication, or a deliberate weight-loss plan. Those portions are set by a vet for one dog and would mislead anyone else. We also left out free-fed dogs (bowls kept full all day) and home-cooked meals without a weight, because there was no daily amount to compare. One owner gave daily calories instead of cups; that report appears in the calorie section below.

What this tally can't tell you

These are self-reports from engaged owners, not a measured study. Two of the 17 dogs were described by their own owners as a little heavy, and nobody listed their brand's calories per cup. Use the table to see what is normal among owners, then fine-tune with your own bag and your own dog.

Why does the amount barely change with weight?

Because owners adjust the scoop to the dog in front of them, not to the scale. In our tally, a 6 lb Pom and a 9.5 lb Pom were both eating 1/2 cup a day, and the most common amount stayed the same from about 6 to 10 lb.

One household described this perfectly. Their 6 lb girl and their 9 lb boy each get 1/4 cup twice a day of the same food. She "is always starving" and wakes them up at 5:30 for breakfast; he "never asks for food". Same portion, different dogs, different appetites.

A second owner with four Poms gave the clearest weight ladder in the data, saying all four stay at an ideal weight with lots of exercise and a few daily treats:

  • 5.5 lb: 2 slightly heaping tablespoons morning and evening
  • 7 lb: 3 slightly heaping tablespoons morning and evening
  • 8 lb: 1/4 cup level, morning and evening
  • 9.5 lb: 1/4 cup level, morning and evening

Notice that the jump from 5.5 lb to 8 lb doubles the food, but 8 lb and 9.5 lb get the same amount. Body frame matters too. Adult Pomeranians vary a lot in size, as our owner-reported adult weight data shows, and a bigger-boned 10 lb Pom is not the same as a round 10 lb Pom.

Activity is the other half

The four-Pom owner above mentioned daily walks and trips to the dog park to play fetch. Another owner said their dog "eats so much food" but stays slim thanks to plenty of exercise, including agility. If your Pom's day looks more like a short stroll, compare it with how long other owners walk their Poms before deciding a portion is too big or too small.

How many calories does a Pomeranian need?

For an average healthy adult dog in ideal condition, the World Small Animal Veterinary Association lists about 140–177 kcal a day at 4.4 lb (2 kg), 190–239 kcal at 6.6 lb (3 kg) and 240–297 kcal at 8.8 lb (4 kg). The chart itself says these are guidance only, because some dogs need more or less.

The WSAVA calorie chart for healthy adult dogs is a one-page table in kilograms. Here it is for Pomeranian sizes in pounds. The in-between weights are our straight-line estimates between the chart's rows.

How do you turn calories into cups?

Divide the calories your dog needs by the kcal per cup printed on your bag. Pet food labels have to show this: according to AAFCO's guide to reading pet food labels, the calorie statement sits under a "Calorie Content" heading and is given per kilogram and per familiar unit, such as per cup.

A simple worked example

Say your Pom weighs 6.6 lb (190–239 kcal a day on the chart) and your food has 380 kcal per cup. 190 ÷ 380 = 0.5 cup, and 239 ÷ 380 is about 0.63 cup. So the chart's starting point is roughly 1/2 to 5/8 cup a day, and treats come out of that total. If the same dog ate a food with more calories per cup, the cup amount would drop.

This is why owners' cups can't simply be copied. One owner in the threads recalled that their other dogs' food has about 450 kcal per cup and their Pom's food about 290 kcal per cup. The same 1/4 cup scoop can carry very different amounts of energy.

Why feeding charts disagree

Bag charts and online charts are written for one food or for an average food, and AAFCO itself calls feeding directions "guidelines that may need modification to meet a particular pet's needs". Several owners said they ignore bag charts entirely; others follow them and are happy. Both groups land in the same place by watching the dog, not the chart.

Step by step: set your Pom's starting portion

  1. Weigh your Pom. Use a pet scale at the vet or weigh yourself holding the dog, then subtract your own weight.
  2. Find the calorie range. Use the WSAVA table above or the checker at the top of this page.
  3. Read your bag's kcal per cup. Look for the "Calorie Content" heading.
  4. Divide. Calories per day ÷ kcal per cup = cups per day. Split it into two meals, as most owners do.
  5. Measure the same way every time. Level the scoop, or follow one owner's advice and weigh portions on a kitchen scale, which is more accurate for small amounts.
  6. Count the extras. Treats, chews and training rewards are food too. If you use lots of rewards, for example during harness training, take a little from the bowl.
  7. Check body condition every few weeks. Feel for the ribs and look for a waist from above, as in the video. Adjust in small steps, such as a tablespoon a day.

Common feeding mistakes owners describe

  • Copying another owner's scoop. 1/4 cup of one food is not 1/4 cup of another. Compare calories, not cups.
  • Forgetting hidden food. One owner found out their spouse was a "secret overtreater". Another's Pom put on weight from chicken nuggets the kids dropped on the floor.
  • Judging by fluff. A full coat hides the body. Several owners recommend feeling for the ribs instead of judging by looks, and one vet-taught tip in the threads was simple: if you have to dig for the ribs, the dog is carrying extra weight.
  • Heaping the scoop. In small portions, "slightly heaping" versus "level" is a real difference. The four-Pom owner above specifies which one for each dog.
  • Changing too much at once. Switching food, portion and treats in the same week makes it impossible to see which change mattered.
  • Using a single number as a goal. Owners of 12–14 lb Poms said their vets considered those weights fine for a larger frame. Ask your vet about your dog's shape rather than chasing a chart weight.

Frequently asked questions

How many cups should a Pomeranian eat a day?

In our 2026 owner tally, the most common amount was 1/2 cup of dry food a day, split into two meals, for Poms of about 6–10 lb. The right amount for your dog depends on your food's calories per cup and your dog's activity.

How many calories does a 7 lb Pomeranian need?

Based on the WSAVA chart for healthy adult dogs, about 200–250 kcal a day is a typical starting range at 7 lb (our estimate between the 6.6 lb and 8.8 lb rows). Individual dogs can need more or less.

Should I feed my Pom once or twice a day?

Nearly every owner in our tally split the daily amount into two meals, and a few used three. Splitting the portion makes small amounts easier to measure and matches what most owners report.

Do treats count toward the daily amount?

Yes. Treats, chews and table food add calories. Owners who keep their Poms steady mention keeping treats few, small or easy to break into tiny pieces.

Why is my Pom always hungry on the same portion as another dog?

Appetite and need are not the same thing. One owner feeds a 6 lb and a 9 lb Pom the same 1/4 cup twice a day; one begs at 5:30 every morning and the other never asks. Judge the portion by body condition, not by begging.

Is the feeding chart on the bag wrong?

Not wrong, but general. AAFCO describes feeding directions as guidelines that may need adjusting for the individual pet. Use the chart to start, then adjust using body condition.

Keep it simple

When you wonder how much to feed a Pomeranian, start with three numbers: your dog's weight, the calorie range for that weight and the kcal per cup on your bag. Most owners end up near 1/2 cup a day for a 6–10 lb Pom, but your bag and your dog have the final word. Measure the same way every day, count the treats, and check the ribs and waist every few weeks. If you're ever unsure about your dog's shape, your vet can check it in person.
